# PM Case Study Builder

A Claude Code skill that turns raw PM work experience into a portfolio-ready case study. Give it a project, a feature, a 0-to-1 build, a hard decision, a platform redesign, and it produces three formats: a full written case study, a condensed LinkedIn article, and a talking-points outline for interviews.

## What it does

- Finds the narrative spine: the non-obvious decision, the real constraint, the outcome that proves something
- Writes a full case study structured around judgment, not feature announcements
- Produces a 600–900 word LinkedIn article version
- Produces a talking-points outline for verbal storytelling in interviews
- Offers to tailor, tighten, or reframe for a specific role or company

## Who it's for

Product managers building a portfolio, prepping for interviews, or trying to turn past work into something they can actually talk about. Especially useful for PMs who know what they built but struggle to write about it compellingly.

## How to use it

Install via [Claude Code](https://claude.ai/code). Drop the `SKILL.md` into your `~/.claude/skills/pm-case-study-builder/` directory.

Trigger it with:

> "Help me write a case study about the onboarding redesign I led at my last company."
> "Turn this project into a portfolio piece."
> "I need to write up my work on the fraud detection platform for interviews."

Then describe the project, messy notes, bullet points, or a full brief all work. The skill will ask the right questions to surface the story.

## Output structure

1. Full written case study (problem → context → what I did → outcome → what I'd do differently)
2. LinkedIn article version (600–900 words, opens with the "so what")
3. Talking-points outline (setup → non-obvious decision → outcome → lesson)
4. Offer to tailor for a specific role, sharpen metrics framing, or cut for a portfolio walkthrough

## What makes a strong PM case study

The skill centers the narrative on the **non-obvious decision**, the moment where multiple reasonable paths existed and judgment was required. That's what a hiring panel actually wants to hear about. Activity descriptions ("I ran standups, I managed the backlog") get cut. What you decided, what you considered and ruled out, and what changed as a result, that's the work.
